

## Immune pathway signatures associated with sepsis

To characterize the biological functions represented by the sepsis susceptibility gene signature, we compared the sepsis-associated genes against the Reactome Pathways library {Milacic, 2023, The Reactome Pathway}. The sepsis signature showed strong enrichment for immune signaling pathways that are central to host inflammatory responses. The highest-ranked pathway was Interleukin-4 and Interleukin-13 Signaling (distance: 1.861, cosine similarity: 0.906), which is involved in anti-inflammatory regulation and alternative macrophage activation. This was followed by Interleukin-10 Signaling (distance: 2.334, cosine similarity: 0.901), a pathway known to suppress excessive cytokine production during systemic infection. The third-ranked pathway was Chemokine Receptors Bind Chemokines (distance: 3.252, cosine similarity: 0.838), reflecting mechanisms of leukocyte trafficking to infection sites.

Additional high-ranking pathways further illustrated the dual immune nature of sepsis. Cytokine signaling networks were broadly represented, including Signaling by Interleukins (distance: 3.651, cosine similarity: 0.807), Cytokine Signaling in Immune System (distance: 4.180, cosine similarity: 0.780), and Interleukin-6 Family Signaling (distance: 3.774, cosine similarity: 0.795), consistent with activation of acute inflammatory and acute phase responses.

Innate immune sensing pathways were also strongly represented. Toll-like receptor cascades were identified, including Toll-like receptor signaling (distance: 4.302, cosine similarity: 0.743) and Toll-like receptor 4 signaling (distance: 4.569, cosine similarity: 0.718), highlighting pathogen recognition mechanisms that initiate septic inflammation. Interferon-γ signaling (distance: 4.276, cosine similarity: 0.762) and inflammasome-related pathways (distance: 4.351, cosine similarity: 0.753) were also enriched, reflecting additional innate immune activation processes.

Collectively, these findings indicate that the sepsis genetic signature captures coordinated activation of pathogen recognition, cytokine signaling, and leukocyte recruitment pathways, consistent with the dysregulated systemic immune response observed clinically in sepsis.
