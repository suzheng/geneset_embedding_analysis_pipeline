## Discussion

The central challenge in sepsis research has been less about identifying candidate targets and more about understanding how those targets interact within a patient who is simultaneously fighting infection and experiencing immune-mediated tissue injury {van der Poll, 2017, The immunopathology of}. Using a gene representation-based framework applied to 23 genetically validated sepsis susceptibility genes, our study provides an integrated view of immune dysregulation and suggests therapeutic strategies that account for this complexity.

### The dual immune signature reflects clinical reality

The most clinically relevant finding is that the sepsis genetic signature reflects both hyperinflammation and immune exhaustion as coexisting biological states {Hotchkiss, 2013, Immunosuppression in sepsis:}. The functional similarity to Th17-driven inflammation and myeloid activation (including monocyte and M1 macrophage programs), alongside CD4+ and CD8+ T cell exhaustion consistent with sepsis-associated immunoparalysis, mirrors clinical observations where patients develop organ-damaging inflammation while failing to clear infection {van der Poll, 2017, The immunopathology of}.

This duality may explain many past clinical trial failures {Marshall, 2014, Why have clinical}. Most trials targeted single inflammatory mediators without considering patient immune status. If inflammation and immune suppression coexist within the same genetic architecture, then targeting only one process would likely produce limited survival benefit. Therefore, therapeutic strategies should focus on restoring immune balance rather than simply suppressing or stimulating immunity.

Pathway analysis supports this view. Both anti-inflammatory pathways such as IL-10 and IL-4/IL-13 signaling and pro-inflammatory cascades including TLR4, IL-6 family, and chemokine signaling were simultaneously enriched {van der Poll, 2017, The immunopathology of}. This pattern suggests a highly activated but poorly regulated immune response rather than a purely inflammatory disease state.

### The IPF connection suggests organ injury and repair dysregulation

We also observed molecular similarity between sepsis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). This relationship becomes biologically intuitive when considering that both diseases involve tissue injury followed by abnormal repair responses. In sepsis, this is particularly relevant to acute respiratory distress syndrome, which shares features with early fibrotic lung disease, including epithelial injury and abnormal fibroblast activation {Burnham, 2013, The fibroproliferative response}. Enrichment of nonspecific interstitial pneumonia signatures further supports this connection. These findings suggest that antifibrotic therapies may be worth exploring during the recovery phase of sepsis-related lung injury, when tissue remodeling becomes dominant {Spagnolo, 2020, Pulmonary fibrosis secondary}.

### JAK inhibition as a mechanistically supported therapeutic strategy

The strongest actionable insight is the convergence of drug signatures supporting JAK-STAT pathway inhibition. Ruxolitinib emerged as a top candidate in drug perturbation analysis, consistent with pathway enrichment of IL-6 and interferon signaling, both of which signal through JAK-STAT pathways {Hu, 2021, The JAK/STAT signaling}. JAK inhibition could potentially reduce both inflammatory signaling and immune exhaustion caused by chronic cytokine exposure {Biwott, 2025, Ruxolitinib Modulates P-Glycoprotein}.

Clinical experience supports this hypothesis. JAK inhibitors have reduced mortality and ventilator dependence in cytokine storm conditions, including selected COVID-19 patients treated with baricitinib or ruxolitinib {Kalil, 2021, Baricitinib plus Remdesivir}. Although sepsis and COVID-19 are clinically distinct, their shared inflammatory signaling patterns provide biological justification for testing JAK inhibition in sepsis patients with hyperinflammatory phenotypes {Olwal, 2021, Parallels in Sepsis}.

Metabolic pathway-associated compounds such as glutamine, calcitriol, and mevalonic acid were also enriched, highlighting the role of immune cell metabolic reprogramming in sepsis pathophysiology {Koutroulis, 2019, Sepsis Immunometabolism: From; Xian, 2026, Metabolic reprogramming tailors}. In the drug perturbation library, glutamine ranked as the strongest match, consistent with the idea that sepsis involves profound shifts in immune cell nutrient utilization and stress adaptation. At the same time, clinical supplementation strategies in critical illness have reported heterogeneous outcomes, emphasizing that metabolic “support” is likely phenotype- and timing-dependent rather than universally beneficial {Heyland, 2013, A Randomized Trial}.

Calcitriol matches point to vitamin D–linked immune regulation as another plausible axis of sepsis vulnerability and recovery {Weiss, 2023, In Vitro and}. The recurrence of broadly immunoregulatory perturbations—including natural products such as curcumin and berberine {Allegra, 2022, The Impact of}—reinforces that multiple intervention classes can map onto the same sepsis-associated functional states. Beyond metabolic modulators, the presence of epigenetic drugs (e.g., azacitidine and decitabine) and proteasome inhibition (bortezomib) among high-similarity perturbations suggests that transcriptional plasticity and protein-homeostasis programs may also contribute to immune dysfunction {Carson, 2011, Epigenetic regulation of; Beltrán-García, 2020, Epigenetic Biomarkers for}. Importantly, some high-similarity compounds are not straightforward repurposing candidates for sepsis (e.g., cytotoxic or immunosuppressive agents such as temozolomide), underscoring that perturbation similarity is best interpreted as a mechanistic pointer to shared immune states rather than a direct treatment recommendation.

### Methodological considerations and limitations

A key strength of this study is that embedding-based methods can identify functional relationships without requiring gene symbol overlap, enabling meaningful analysis of a relatively small gene set. Variance-based filtering improved biological signal detection by retaining dimensions that reflected shared functional characteristics. The 91.3% gene coverage further supports the robustness of the derived sepsis signature.

However, the analysis focuses on susceptibility genes rather than those driving disease severity or organ failure {Weng, 2025, KEY GENETIC DETERMINANTS}. In addition, drug signature databases are derived from controlled experimental systems and may not fully represent in vivo septic conditions {Otava, 2015, Identification of in}. Therefore, our results should be interpreted as mechanistic hypotheses requiring experimental and clinical validation.

### Conclusion

The sepsis genetic architecture reflects simultaneous inflammatory activation and immune exhaustion, explaining why single-target immunomodulatory therapies have had limited success. Molecular similarity with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis highlights tissue remodeling as an important but underexplored aspect of sepsis biology. The consistent identification of JAK-STAT pathway inhibition across analyses provides a biologically coherent and clinically testable therapeutic hypothesis. Overall, these findings reinforce that sepsis represents a systemic failure of immune regulation across multiple biological axes, requiring multidimensional therapeutic approaches.
