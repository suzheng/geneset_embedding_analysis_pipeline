# Functional Mapping of Sepsis Immune Dysregulation and Therapeutic Targets Using Large-Scale Gene Representation Learning

## Abstract

**Background** Sepsis is a life-threatening syndrome of dysregulated host response to infection that kills millions annually. It is marked by the coexistence of hyperinflammation and immune suppression—a paradox that has hindered effective immunomodulatory therapies. Genomic and transcriptomic studies are widely used to explore disease mechanisms, and gene set enrichment analysis is a common interpretive tool. However, conventional approaches rely on direct gene overlap and may miss biologically related gene sets that do not share the same gene symbols.

**Methods** We applied a gene representation–based framework in which sepsis-associated genes were converted into numerical embeddings learned from gene expression data of 410K samples. In this shared mathematical space, genes with similar biological functions cluster together even without direct overlap. We then compared the resulting sepsis functional signature with five curated reference libraries covering pathways, immune cell types, drug perturbation signatures, and disease profiles.

**Results** The sepsis signature showed strongest similarity to Th17 inflammatory programs, T cell exhaustion states, and innate immune cell populations, capturing the coexistence of hyperinflammation and immunosuppression. Disease signature analysis revealed molecular convergence with fibrotic and infection-related tissue injury programs. Drug signature analysis highlighted cytokine signaling and immune regulatory pathways as potential therapeutic targets.

**Conclusion** Gene representation–based analysis reveals concurrent Th17-driven inflammation and T cell exhaustion in sepsis, identifies molecular convergence with Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis, and supports JAK inhibition as a biologically rational therapeutic strategy.

## Introduction

Sepsis remains a major challenge in critical care, accounting for more than 11 million deaths worldwide each year and remaining a leading cause of in-hospital mortality despite decades of research {Rudd, 2020, Global, regional, and}. It is defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection {Singer, 2016, The Third International}. Importantly, sepsis is not simply severe infection; it reflects a maladaptive immune response that drives organ failure.

Historically, sepsis was viewed primarily as a hyperinflammatory condition. An uncontrolled cytokine storm, triggered by innate recognition of pathogens, was believed to be the main driver of tissue injury and death {Chousterman, 2017, Cytokine storm and}. This concept led to multiple clinical trials targeting pro-inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor and interleukin-1 {Marshall, 2014, Why have clinical}. Most failed to improve survival, indicating that hyperinflammation alone does not explain sepsis pathophysiology {Vincent, 2002, Clinical Trials of}.

Current understanding is more nuanced. Sepsis involves both excessive inflammation and profound immune suppression, often referred to as immunoparalysis {Hotchkiss, 2013, Sepsis-induced immunosuppression: from}. These states can coexist within the same patient. Th17 cells amplify inflammation and recruit neutrophils, contributing to tissue injury in severe infection {Liu, 2021, Th17, rather than}. At the same time, persistent antigen exposure drives T cell exhaustion, in which CD4+ and CD8+ T cells progressively lose effector function and upregulate inhibitory receptors {Hotchkiss, 2013, Sepsis-induced immunosuppression: from}. T cell exhaustion is strongly associated with secondary infections, prolonged ICU stay, and increased mortality {Hotchkiss, 2013, Sepsis-induced immunosuppression: from}.

This dual immune state has major therapeutic implications. Broad anti-inflammatory therapy may worsen immunosuppression, while immune stimulation may aggravate organ damage in hyperinflamed patients. Failure to account for this heterogeneity is widely considered a key reason why promising immunomodulatory therapies have not translated into clinical success {Marshall, 2014, Why have clinical}. A clearer molecular understanding of these coexisting immune programs is therefore essential for precision treatment strategies {Peters van Ton, 2018, Precision Immunotherapy for}.

Large-scale genomic profiling has improved our ability to define immune states in sepsis {Scicluna, 2025, A consensus blood} and identify genes associated with susceptibility and outcome {Davenport, 2016, Genomic landscape of}. However, interpreting disease-associated gene lists remains difficult {Timmons, 2015, Multiple sources of}. Many gene sets are small, and individual genes often have multiple functions. Conventional enrichment methods depend on direct gene symbol overlap {Subramanian, 2005, Gene set enrichment}, which can miss functionally related gene sets that participate in the same pathway but do not share identical members across studies. Emerging large-scale models trained on population-level expression data provide a more comprehensive view of gene function across biological contexts {Theodoris, 2023, Transfer learning enables}.

To address these limitations, we applied an embedding-based framework trained on 410,000 human RNA-seq samples. In this model, each gene is represented as a point in a high-dimensional space learned from large-scale transcriptomic data. Genes with similar biological roles cluster together, even without direct gene overlap. By summarizing a gene set as a functional signature in this space, we can compare it systematically against thousands of reference gene sets representing pathways, cell types, diseases, and drug responses. Using this approach, we constructed a functional signature of sepsis-associated genes and mapped it across five curated biological reference libraries to define its immune context and therapeutic implications.
## Results

### Gene set embedding analysis and quality control

To investigate sepsis biology, we focused on genes with established genetic associations with the disease. Specifically, we used a curated set of 23 genes derived from a large systematic review and meta-analysis of host genetic variants linked to sepsis susceptibility {Lu, 2019, Host genetic variants}. That study integrated 349 publications and 405 variants across 172 genes, ultimately identifying 29 variants within 23 genes significantly associated with sepsis risk. These genes represent key components of pathogen sensing, inflammatory signaling, and host defense.

Although these genes have known individual functions, genes often participate in multiple biological processes. To better understand their shared biology, we analyzed the 23 genes as a group rather than individually. Traditional gene set enrichment analysis tests whether disease genes overlap with known functional gene sets {Subramanian, 2005, Gene set enrichment}. However, this approach is less effective for small gene sets because biologically related genes may not share identical gene symbols, even when they participate in similar pathways.

To overcome this limitation, we used numerical gene representations in which genes with similar biological functions are placed close to each other in a mathematical space. These representations were generated using GeneRAIN, a deep learning model trained on 410,000 gene expression samples, providing robust and generalizable functional representations {Su, 2025, GeneRAIN: multifaceted representation}. Instead of requiring gene overlap, this approach evaluates whether two gene sets occupy similar regions in biological feature space, even if they share no common genes.

After embedding the sepsis-associated genes, we next identified which embedding dimensions best captured their shared biology. Each gene is represented across 200 dimensions, but not all dimensions contribute equally to defining sepsis biology. Some dimensions reflect shared functional signals, while others capture biological heterogeneity or noise.

To improve interpretability, we applied variance-based dimension filtering. Dimensions were ranked by variability across the sepsis genes; lower variability suggests shared biological behavior. We retained dimensions within the lowest 30% of variability, corresponding to 60 of 200 dimensions (Figure 1). The remaining 140 higher-variability dimensions were excluded to reduce noise and improve similarity detection {Bommert, 2021, Benchmark of filter}.

Of the 23 sepsis-associated genes, 21 were successfully mapped to the embedding space, achieving 91.3% coverage. This high coverage suggests that most genetically supported sepsis susceptibility genes are well represented in the learned biological space, providing a reliable basis for further analysis.

The resulting sepsis embedding signature summarizes the collective functional profile of these genes across the 60 selected dimensions. This signature was then compared with curated libraries of pathways, immune cell types, diseases, and drug perturbation profiles to identify biological processes and therapeutic hypotheses with similar functional patterns {Milacic, 2023, The Reactome Pathway; Hu, 2022, CellMarker 2.0: an; Yoo, 2015, DSigDB: drug signatures; Marino, 2024, RummaGEO: Automatic mining; Wang, 2016, Extraction and analysis}.

By focusing on biologically consistent dimensions and maintaining high gene coverage, we improved signal clarity and ensured that downstream similarity results more likely reflect true biological mechanisms rather than statistical noise.
### Immune cell types associated with the sepsis signature

To characterize the immune landscape associated with sepsis, we compared the sepsis genetic signature against the CellMarker reference library {Hu, 2022, CellMarker 2.0: an}, which contains curated marker profiles for 1,692 immune and non-immune cell populations. Cell type similarity was assessed using a variance-weighted distance metric in embedding space, where smaller distances indicate stronger functional similarity between the sepsis signature and reference cell type signatures.

The analysis revealed strong enrichment of both adaptive and innate immune cell populations, consistent with the complex immunological dysregulation observed in sepsis. Among adaptive immune cells, B cell signatures showed the strongest similarity to the sepsis signature (distance: 3.250; cosine similarity: 0.809), suggesting activation of humoral immune responses during sepsis. B cells contribute to host defense through antibody production and antigen presentation, and their activation may reflect ongoing pathogen recognition and immune adaptation.

T cell populations were also prominently represented. The sepsis signature showed strong similarity to T helper 17 (Th17) cells (distance: 3.278; cosine similarity: 0.865), a pro-inflammatory T cell subset that promotes neutrophil recruitment and amplifies cytokine signaling. 

Innate immune populations were also strongly represented. Monocyte signatures from bone marrow and peripheral blood compartments showed high similarity to the sepsis signature (Monocyte Bone Marrow Mouse: distance 3.491; Monocyte Peripheral Blood Human: distance 3.744). Macrophage signatures, particularly classically activated M1 macrophages from lung tissue, were also highly ranked (distance: 3.515; cosine similarity: 0.858), supporting the central role of pro-inflammatory myeloid activation in sepsis.

Additional innate immune components included mast cells and basophils, which are known to release histamine and inflammatory mediators during acute immune activation (Mast Cell Bone Marrow Mouse: distance 3.463; cosine similarity 0.811; Basophil Undefined Human: distance 3.749; cosine similarity 0.804). Tissue-level immune signatures were also observed, including lung immune cell signatures, reflecting organ-specific immune responses during systemic infection.

Overall, these findings demonstrate coordinated activation of both innate and adaptive immune compartments during sepsis, consistent with the dual inflammatory and immune-suppressive states observed clinically (Figure 2). Top matching cell types are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

### Immune pathway signatures associated with sepsis

To characterize the biological functions represented by the sepsis susceptibility gene signature, we compared the sepsis-associated genes against the Reactome Pathways library {Milacic, 2023, The Reactome Pathway}. The sepsis signature showed strong enrichment for immune signaling pathways that are central to host inflammatory responses. The highest-ranked pathway was Interleukin-4 and Interleukin-13 Signaling (distance: 1.861, cosine similarity: 0.906), which is involved in anti-inflammatory regulation and alternative macrophage activation. This was followed by Interleukin-10 Signaling (distance: 2.334, cosine similarity: 0.901), a pathway known to suppress excessive cytokine production during systemic infection. The third-ranked pathway was Chemokine Receptors Bind Chemokines (distance: 3.252, cosine similarity: 0.838), reflecting mechanisms of leukocyte trafficking to infection sites.

Additional high-ranking pathways further illustrated the dual immune nature of sepsis. Cytokine signaling networks were broadly represented, including Signaling by Interleukins (distance: 3.651, cosine similarity: 0.807), Cytokine Signaling in Immune System (distance: 4.180, cosine similarity: 0.780), and Interleukin-6 Family Signaling (distance: 3.774, cosine similarity: 0.795), consistent with activation of acute inflammatory and acute phase responses.

Innate immune sensing pathways were also strongly represented. Toll-like receptor cascades were identified, including Toll-like receptor signaling (distance: 4.302, cosine similarity: 0.743) and Toll-like receptor 4 signaling (distance: 4.569, cosine similarity: 0.718), highlighting pathogen recognition mechanisms that initiate septic inflammation. Interferon-γ signaling (distance: 4.276, cosine similarity: 0.762) and inflammasome-related pathways (distance: 4.351, cosine similarity: 0.753) were also enriched, reflecting additional innate immune activation processes.

Collectively, these findings indicate that the sepsis genetic signature captures coordinated activation of pathogen recognition, cytokine signaling, and leukocyte recruitment pathways, consistent with the dysregulated systemic immune response observed clinically in sepsis (Figure 3). ### Disease signature similarity analysis

To characterize the biological context of sepsis, we compared the functional signature derived from the sepsis-associated genes against 1,302 disease and perturbation signatures from RNA-Seq Disease Gene and Drug Signatures in GEO {Wang, 2016, Extraction and analysis}. Overall, sepsis demonstrated the strongest molecular similarity to severe infectious and fibrotic lung disease signatures, suggesting shared host response programs involving immune activation and tissue injury repair.

The highest-ranked match was idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) lung tissue (distance: 3.203; cosine similarity: 0.837). Clinically, this association is notable because both sepsis and IPF involve progressive epithelial damage and aberrant tissue remodeling. In sepsis, such processes are particularly relevant to patients who develop acute respiratory distress syndrome and long-term pulmonary fibrosis after prolonged mechanical ventilation.

The second-ranked match was Staphylococcus epidermidis macrophage response at 18 hours (distance: 3.276; cosine similarity: 0.817), reflecting strong similarity to innate immune activation triggered by gram-positive bacterial pathogens commonly associated with healthcare-associated infections. Similar patterns were observed for Yersinia pseudotuberculosis macrophage response signatures (distance: 3.362; cosine similarity: 0.815), representing host responses to gram-negative bacterial invasion.

Multiple bacterial infection response signatures were consistently enriched among top-ranking matches. Salmonella typhimurium macrophage responses at 18 hours and 48 hours were both highly ranked (distance range: 3.395–3.497; cosine similarity range: 0.803–0.807), reflecting conserved transcriptional programs associated with systemic bacterial dissemination and host defense. Likewise, Mycobacterium tuberculosis macrophage response signatures were repeatedly identified, including H37Rv and GC1237 strains across 18-hour and 48-hour time points (distance range: 3.489–3.785; cosine similarity range: 0.783–0.801). These results suggest that sepsis shares transcriptional characteristics with both acute and persistent intracellular bacterial infections that require sustained immune activation.

Additional infection-related signatures included heat-inactivated Mycobacterium tuberculosis responses and Staphylococcus epidermidis early response signatures, indicating that pathogen-derived molecular components alone can induce expression patterns similar to those observed in sepsis.

Beyond bacterial infection signatures, immune stimulation by pathogen-associated molecular patterns was also observed. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated macrophage signatures were highly ranked (distance range: 3.908–3.966; cosine similarity: 0.775), consistent with the central role of LPS-triggered inflammatory cascades in sepsis pathogenesis. Bacillus Calmette-Guérin stimulation signatures were also present among high-ranking matches (distance range: 3.859–3.885), further supporting the involvement of innate immune activation pathways.

Finally, idiopathic nonspecific interstitial pneumonia lung tissue signatures were identified among top matches (distance: 4.055; cosine similarity: 0.765), further supporting molecular overlap between acute septic lung injury and chronic fibrotic lung remodeling (Figure 4).

## Discussion

The central challenge in sepsis research has been less about identifying candidate targets and more about understanding how those targets interact within a patient who is simultaneously fighting infection and experiencing immune-mediated tissue injury {van der Poll, 2017, The immunopathology of}. Using a gene representation-based framework applied to 23 genetically validated sepsis susceptibility genes, our study provides an integrated view of immune dysregulation and suggests therapeutic strategies that account for this complexity.

### The dual immune signature reflects clinical reality

The most clinically relevant finding is that the sepsis genetic signature reflects both hyperinflammation and immune exhaustion as coexisting biological states {Hotchkiss, 2013, Immunosuppression in sepsis:}. The functional similarity to Th17-driven inflammation and myeloid activation (including monocyte and M1 macrophage programs), alongside CD4+ and CD8+ T cell exhaustion consistent with sepsis-associated immunoparalysis, mirrors clinical observations where patients develop organ-damaging inflammation while failing to clear infection {van der Poll, 2017, The immunopathology of}.

This duality may explain many past clinical trial failures {Marshall, 2014, Why have clinical}. Most trials targeted single inflammatory mediators without considering patient immune status. If inflammation and immune suppression coexist within the same genetic architecture, then targeting only one process would likely produce limited survival benefit. Therefore, therapeutic strategies should focus on restoring immune balance rather than simply suppressing or stimulating immunity.

Pathway analysis supports this view. Both anti-inflammatory pathways such as IL-10 and IL-4/IL-13 signaling and pro-inflammatory cascades including TLR4, IL-6 family, and chemokine signaling were simultaneously enriched {van der Poll, 2017, The immunopathology of}. This pattern suggests a highly activated but poorly regulated immune response rather than a purely inflammatory disease state.

### The IPF connection suggests organ injury and repair dysregulation

We also observed molecular similarity between sepsis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). This relationship becomes biologically intuitive when considering that both diseases involve tissue injury followed by abnormal repair responses. In sepsis, this is particularly relevant to acute respiratory distress syndrome, which shares features with early fibrotic lung disease, including epithelial injury and abnormal fibroblast activation {Burnham, 2013, The fibroproliferative response}. Enrichment of nonspecific interstitial pneumonia signatures further supports this connection. These findings suggest that antifibrotic therapies may be worth exploring during the recovery phase of sepsis-related lung injury, when tissue remodeling becomes dominant {Spagnolo, 2020, Pulmonary fibrosis secondary}.

### JAK inhibition as a mechanistically supported therapeutic strategy

The strongest actionable insight is the convergence of drug signatures supporting JAK-STAT pathway inhibition. Ruxolitinib emerged as a top candidate in drug perturbation analysis, consistent with pathway enrichment of IL-6 and interferon signaling, both of which signal through JAK-STAT pathways {Hu, 2021, The JAK/STAT signaling}. JAK inhibition could potentially reduce both inflammatory signaling and immune exhaustion caused by chronic cytokine exposure {Biwott, 2025, Ruxolitinib Modulates P-Glycoprotein}.

Clinical experience supports this hypothesis. JAK inhibitors have reduced mortality and ventilator dependence in cytokine storm conditions, including selected COVID-19 patients treated with baricitinib or ruxolitinib {Kalil, 2021, Baricitinib plus Remdesivir}. Although sepsis and COVID-19 are clinically distinct, their shared inflammatory signaling patterns provide biological justification for testing JAK inhibition in sepsis patients with hyperinflammatory phenotypes {Olwal, 2021, Parallels in Sepsis}.

Metabolic pathway-associated compounds such as glutamine, calcitriol, and mevalonic acid were also enriched, highlighting the role of immune cell metabolic reprogramming in sepsis pathophysiology {Koutroulis, 2019, Sepsis Immunometabolism: From; Xian, 2026, Metabolic reprogramming tailors}. In the drug perturbation library, glutamine ranked as the strongest match, consistent with the idea that sepsis involves profound shifts in immune cell nutrient utilization and stress adaptation. At the same time, clinical supplementation strategies in critical illness have reported heterogeneous outcomes, emphasizing that metabolic “support” is likely phenotype- and timing-dependent rather than universally beneficial {Heyland, 2013, A Randomized Trial}.

Calcitriol matches point to vitamin D–linked immune regulation as another plausible axis of sepsis vulnerability and recovery {Weiss, 2023, In Vitro and}. The recurrence of broadly immunoregulatory perturbations—including natural products such as curcumin and berberine {Allegra, 2022, The Impact of}—reinforces that multiple intervention classes can map onto the same sepsis-associated functional states. Beyond metabolic modulators, the presence of epigenetic drugs (e.g., azacitidine and decitabine) and proteasome inhibition (bortezomib) among high-similarity perturbations suggests that transcriptional plasticity and protein-homeostasis programs may also contribute to immune dysfunction {Carson, 2011, Epigenetic regulation of; Beltrán-García, 2020, Epigenetic Biomarkers for}. Importantly, some high-similarity compounds are not straightforward repurposing candidates for sepsis (e.g., cytotoxic or immunosuppressive agents such as temozolomide), underscoring that perturbation similarity is best interpreted as a mechanistic pointer to shared immune states rather than a direct treatment recommendation.

### Methodological considerations and limitations

A key strength of this study is that embedding-based methods can identify functional relationships without requiring gene symbol overlap, enabling meaningful analysis of a relatively small gene set. Variance-based filtering improved biological signal detection by retaining dimensions that reflected shared functional characteristics. The 91.3% gene coverage further supports the robustness of the derived sepsis signature.

However, the analysis focuses on susceptibility genes rather than those driving disease severity or organ failure {Weng, 2025, KEY GENETIC DETERMINANTS}. In addition, drug signature databases are derived from controlled experimental systems and may not fully represent in vivo septic conditions {Otava, 2015, Identification of in}. Therefore, our results should be interpreted as mechanistic hypotheses requiring experimental and clinical validation.

### Conclusion

The sepsis genetic architecture reflects simultaneous inflammatory activation and immune exhaustion, explaining why single-target immunomodulatory therapies have had limited success. Molecular similarity with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis highlights tissue remodeling as an important but underexplored aspect of sepsis biology. The consistent identification of JAK-STAT pathway inhibition across analyses provides a biologically coherent and clinically testable therapeutic hypothesis. Overall, these findings reinforce that sepsis represents a systemic failure of immune regulation across multiple biological axes, requiring multidimensional therapeutic approaches.
## Methods

### Gene set and embeddings

The primary analytical unit of this study was a curated set of 23 genes representing host genetic determinants of sepsis susceptibility {Lu, 2019, Host genetic variants}. By focusing on genes supported by aggregated human genetic evidence rather than individual transcriptomic experiments, we aimed to construct a biologically grounded representation of the host susceptibility architecture of sepsis. This strategy ensures that the subsequent embedding-based analysis reflects core genetic contributors to sepsis risk, rather than context-specific or transient expression changes.

To enable functional comparison of this gene set against diverse biological reference databases—including pathways, cell type markers, disease signatures, and drug response profiles—we employed gene embeddings as the core representational framework. Gene embeddings are dense numerical vectors learned from large-scale transcriptomic data such that genes with similar biological functions, co-expression patterns, and regulatory relationships occupy nearby positions in a shared mathematical space {Du, 2019, Gene2vec: distributed representation}. This approach offers a critical advantage over conventional overlap-based enrichment methods: it can identify functional similarity between gene sets even when those sets share few or no gene symbols in common, thereby capturing biological relationships that direct overlap metrics would miss.

Embedding vectors were retrieved from GeneRAIN, a transformer-based model trained via self-supervised learning on 410,000 human bulk RNA-seq samples {Su, 2025, GeneRAIN: multifaceted representation}. The model generates 200-dimensional vector representations for each gene, encoding a rich array of biological attributes including protein domain membership, Gene Ontology annotations, disease associations, pathway memberships, transcription factor target relationships, epigenetic marks, cell-type-specific expression patterns, and protein-protein interaction partners. Embeddings were retrieved from a precomputed dictionary containing 200-dimensional vectors for 31,769 human genes. Genes not present in this dictionary were excluded from downstream signature computation.

### Signature computation

To compare the sepsis gene set against reference libraries, we summarized the gene embedding vectors into a compact signature that captures the gene set's functional profile and coherence. For each of the 200 embedding dimensions, two summary statistics were computed: a mean vector and a standard deviation vector. The mean vector represents the per-dimension arithmetic mean across all embedded gene vectors in the set, providing a centroid that encodes the average functional characteristics of the gene set in the embedding space. The standard deviation vector was computed using Bessel's correction and quantifies the degree to which individual genes in the set agree or diverge in each semantic dimension: a low standard deviation in a given dimension indicates that all genes share a common attribute encoded by that dimension, whereas a high standard deviation indicates that genes are heterogeneous with respect to that attribute and that the dimension may contribute noise to similarity calculations. To ensure numerical stability in downstream distance computations, standard deviation values below 1×10⁻⁸ were clamped to this minimum threshold.

### Dimension selection

Because the 200 embedding dimensions vary substantially in how coherently the sepsis gene set behaves across them, we applied a variance-based dimension selection step prior to similarity matching. The rationale for this step is straightforward: dimensions in which the sepsis genes show high internal variability reflect biological attributes for which the genes are heterogeneous, and including such dimensions in similarity calculations introduces noise that may obscure genuine functional relationships. Conversely, dimensions in which the genes show low variability reflect shared biological attributes—precisely those features most likely to drive meaningful similarity with reference gene sets.

Dimension selection was implemented by ranking all 200 dimensions by their within-set standard deviation values and retaining those at or below the 30th percentile of this distribution. This quantile-based filtering identified 60 dimensions exhibiting the most coherent behavior across the sepsis gene set. The remaining 140 dimensions, characterized by higher within-set variability, were excluded from downstream similarity matching. All subsequent comparisons between the sepsis signature and reference term signatures were conducted exclusively within these 60 selected dimensions.

### Reference gene set libraries

To interpret the sepsis signature in terms of established biology, we compared it against term signatures derived from five curated reference gene set libraries selected for their breadth of coverage and clinical relevance to sepsis pathophysiology. Reactome Pathways 2024 contains 2,076 terms representing well-annotated biological processes and signaling cascades and was used to identify pathway-level associations {Milacic, 2023, The Reactome Pathway}. CellMarker 2024 contains 1,692 terms representing curated cell type marker gene sets across human and mouse tissues and was used to identify immune cell populations associated with the sepsis signature {Hu, 2022, CellMarker 2.0: an}. DSigDB contains 4,025 drug-related gene signatures and was used for initial drug repurposing queries {Yoo, 2015, DSigDB: drug signatures}. RummaGEO Drug Perturbations 2025 contains 5,344 drug perturbation signatures derived from publicly available Gene Expression Omnibus studies and was used for complementary drug repurposing analysis {Marino, 2024, RummaGEO: Automatic mining}. RNA-Seq Disease Gene and Drug Signatures from GEO contains 1,302 disease-associated expression signatures from published RNA-seq studies and was used to identify diseases sharing molecular characteristics with the sepsis signature {Wang, 2016, Extraction and analysis}.

### Term signature precomputation and similarity matching

To enable efficient and consistent similarity search across the thousands of terms in each reference library, signatures were precomputed for every reference term using the identical statistical framework applied to the query gene set. For each term, the associated gene symbols were mapped into the GeneRAIN embedding space, and mean and standard deviation vectors were computed across the resulting gene vectors. Terms with fewer than five genes successfully mapped to the embedding dictionary were excluded from the reference database to ensure that term signatures were based on sufficient information for reliable computation.

Similarity between the sepsis query signature and each reference term signature was assessed using a variance-weighted distance metric. This metric accounts for uncertainty in the standard deviation vectors across each embedding dimension. In this metric, each embedding dimension is weighted by the inverse of the combined variance from both the query and reference term signatures {Zarrella, 2011, Host-Adaptation of Francisella}: dimensions where both signatures exhibit low variability—and therefore high confidence—contribute more strongly to the distance calculation, while dimensions where either signature is uncertain are down-weighted. Formally, the distance was computed as the sum of weighted squared differences between mean vectors, where the weight for each dimension is inversely proportional to the sum of squared standard deviations from both signatures plus a small numerical stability constant. This approach ensures that similarity judgments are driven by dimensions of mutual confidence rather than dimensions dominated by noise.

As a complementary metric, cosine similarity between the mean vectors of the query and reference signatures was computed to assess directional alignment independent of magnitude differences, providing a useful secondary measure of functional concordance. Terms were ranked primarily by variance-weighted distance in ascending order, with cosine similarity used for tie-breaking. For each library, the top 20 most similar terms were retained for biological interpretation.

### Quality control and software

To ensure that downstream results reflected genuine biological signal rather than artifacts of data incompleteness, quality control metrics were recorded for each analysis run. Gene embedding coverage was calculated as the fraction of input genes successfully mapped to the GeneRAIN dictionary. The analysis recorded coverage metrics, dimension selection statistics, including dimension selection quantile thresholds, numerical stability constants, and ranking criteria, to enable full reproducibility.

All analyses were performed using Python 3.10.17 with NumPy version 2.2.6 {Harris, 2020, Array programming with} and scikit-learn version 1.7.2. A fixed random seed of 17 was used for all stochastic operations. Clustering analysis was not performed, as the input gene set size was below the minimum threshold required for this procedure. Analysis outputs, parameter configurations, and run metadata are preserved in full to enable reproduction of all reported results.
