Results

Gene Set, Embeddings and Quality Control

The analysis was performed on a curated set of 23 sepsis-associated genes representing key mediators of the immune response and inflammatory pathways implicated in sepsis pathophysiology. Gene symbols were normalized through standardized preprocessing procedures, including whitespace trimming and conversion to uppercase, to ensure consistent mapping to the reference embedding space. Following preprocessing, all 23 input genes were processed for embedding retrieval.

Quality control assessment revealed that 17 of the 23 input genes (73.9%) were successfully mapped to the GeneRAIN embedding dictionary, which contains 31,769 genes with precomputed 200-dimensional vector representations. The six genes that could not be mapped were excluded from downstream signature computation. This coverage rate of 73.9% indicates that the majority of sepsis-associated genes in the input set were represented in the embedding space, enabling reliable signature computation. The successfully mapped genes encompassed key immune mediators including pattern recognition receptors, cytokines, chemokines, and other inflammatory signaling molecules central to sepsis pathogenesis.

The 17 successfully mapped genes were converted into their corresponding 200-dimensional embedding vectors, where each dimension represents a learned biological feature derived from analysis of 410,000 human RNA-seq samples. To summarize the collective functional profile of the sepsis gene set, statistical signatures were computed across all embedding dimensions. For each of the 200 dimensions, two summary statistics were calculated: a mean vector representing the centroid position of the gene set in that dimension, and a standard deviation vector capturing the variability of genes within the set. The mean vector provides a measure of the central tendency of the sepsis genes' functional characteristics, while the standard deviation quantifies the coherence or diversity of the gene set across each semantic dimension.

To focus similarity calculations on the most stable and informative embedding dimensions, a variance-based dimension selection approach was applied. Dimensions were ranked by their standard deviation values, and those with variability at or below the 30th percentile threshold were retained for similarity matching. This quantile-based filtering identified 60 dimensions (30% of 200 total dimensions) that exhibited coherent behavior across the sepsis gene set, where genes showed convergent functional characteristics. The remaining 140 dimensions, which displayed higher variability and potentially introduced noise, were excluded from downstream similarity calculations. This dimension selection strategy enhances the signal-to-noise ratio by prioritizing dimensions where the sepsis genes exhibit consistent functional patterns.

The computed embedding signature, consisting of mean and standard deviation vectors across the selected 60 dimensions, provides a quantitative representation of the sepsis gene set's functional profile in the embedding space. This signature serves as the basis for similarity matching against reference gene set libraries, enabling identification of biological pathways, immune cell types, drug signatures, and disease associations that share similar functional characteristics with the sepsis-associated genes.
