# Results

## Comparison to Other Infectious Diseases

To identify disease signatures that share similar gene expression patterns with sepsis, the sepsis-associated gene signature was compared against the RNA-Seq Disease Gene and Drug Signatures from GEO library, which contains 1,302 disease and perturbation signatures derived from publicly available gene expression studies. Disease signature matching was performed using variance-weighted distance calculations across selected embedding dimensions, where smaller distance values indicate greater similarity between the sepsis signature and disease-associated expression patterns. The analysis revealed strong associations with bacterial infection signatures and pulmonary fibrotic diseases, providing insights into shared pathophysiological mechanisms.

The highest-ranked disease signature match was Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis Lung Tissue (distance: 3.203, cosine similarity: 0.837), a chronic fibrotic lung disease characterized by progressive scarring and impaired gas exchange. This finding is clinically relevant as sepsis can lead to acute respiratory distress syndrome and pulmonary fibrosis, particularly in patients with prolonged mechanical ventilation or severe lung injury. The strong similarity suggests that sepsis and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis may share common pathways involving tissue remodeling, inflammation, and fibroblast activation. The second-ranked match was Staphylococcus epidermidis Macrophage 18hr (distance: 3.276, cosine similarity: 0.817), reflecting macrophage activation in response to a gram-positive bacterial pathogen commonly associated with healthcare-associated infections and sepsis. This was followed by Yersinia pseudotuberculosis Macrophage 18hr (distance: 3.362, cosine similarity: 0.815), representing the macrophage response to a gram-negative bacterial pathogen.

Multiple bacterial infection signatures were prominently represented among the top matches. Salmonella typhimurium Macrophage signatures were highly ranked, with Salmonella typhimurium Macrophage 48hr (distance: 3.395, cosine similarity: 0.803) and Salmonella typhimurium Macrophage 18hr (distance: 3.498, cosine similarity: 0.807) both in the top 10. Salmonella is a gram-negative enteric pathogen that can cause bacteremia and sepsis, particularly in immunocompromised patients, making this similarity clinically meaningful. Multiple signatures representing macrophage responses to Mycobacterium tuberculosis were also highly ranked, including Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Rv Macrophage 48hr (distance: 3.489, cosine similarity: 0.801), Mycobacterium tuberculosis GC1237 Macrophage 48hr (distance: 3.666, cosine similarity: 0.790), and Mycobacterium tuberculosis GC1237 Macrophage 18hr (distance: 3.691, cosine similarity: 0.793). These tuberculosis signatures reflect the macrophage response to a persistent intracellular bacterial pathogen, demonstrating that sepsis shares gene expression patterns with chronic bacterial infections that require sustained immune activation. Additional bacterial responses included heat-inactivated Mycobacterium tuberculosis signatures (distance: 3.693, cosine similarity: 0.787), indicating that bacterial components alone can trigger similar gene expression patterns to live pathogens, and Staphylococcus epidermidis at 4hr timepoint (distance: 3.689, cosine similarity: 0.796).

Beyond bacterial infections, additional signatures were identified including Bacillus Calmette-Guerin Macrophage responses (distances ranging from 3.859 to 3.885), representing macrophage activation by the tuberculosis vaccine strain, and Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) stimulation signatures, with LPS Bone Marrow-Derived Macrophage 10 ng/ml 3 hrs (distance: 3.908, cosine similarity: 0.775) and LPS Bone Marrow-Derived Macrophage 10 ng/ml 6 hrs (distance: 3.966, cosine similarity: 0.775) identified. LPS is a major component of gram-negative bacterial cell walls and is a key trigger of the inflammatory response in sepsis, making these matches particularly relevant as they represent the direct response to a sepsis-associated pathogen-associated molecular pattern. Idiopathic nonspecific interstitial pneumonia lung tissue (distance: 4.055, cosine similarity: 0.765) was also identified, another fibrotic lung disease that shares clinical features with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

The disease signature comparison reveals that sepsis shares gene expression patterns with severe bacterial infections and fibrotic lung diseases, providing insights into shared pathophysiological mechanisms. The strong similarity to macrophage responses to bacterial pathogens, including tuberculosis, salmonella, staphylococcus, and yersinia, demonstrates that sepsis activates similar immune pathways as other severe bacterial infections, regardless of the specific pathogen. This finding is clinically relevant as it suggests that therapeutic strategies developed for other bacterial infections may be applicable to sepsis, and that understanding the common immune response pathways could inform treatment approaches. The association with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and nonspecific interstitial pneumonia highlights the pulmonary complications of sepsis, particularly the risk of developing fibrotic lung disease in patients with severe sepsis and acute respiratory distress syndrome. The presence of LPS stimulation signatures among the top matches validates the analysis approach, as LPS is a well-established trigger of sepsis pathophysiology, and demonstrates that the sepsis signature captures responses to bacterial components that drive the inflammatory cascade. These findings support the clinical understanding that sepsis involves activation of conserved immune response pathways that are shared across different bacterial infections, suggesting that targeting these common pathways may provide broad therapeutic benefit regardless of the specific causative pathogen.
