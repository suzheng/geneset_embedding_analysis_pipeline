# Results

## Drug Repurposing Candidates Identified from DSigDB

To identify potential drug repurposing opportunities for sepsis treatment, the sepsis-associated gene signature was compared against the DSigDB library, which contains 4,025 drug-related gene signatures representing compounds with documented effects on gene expression. Drug matching was performed using variance-weighted distance calculations across 60 selected embedding dimensions, where smaller distance values indicate greater similarity between the sepsis signature and drug-induced expression patterns. The analysis revealed strong associations with several classes of anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs that are clinically relevant to sepsis pathophysiology.

The highest-ranked drug match was POVPC (distance: 1.761, cosine similarity: 0.912), a lipid mediator oxidized phosphatidylcholine with anti-inflammatory properties. This was followed by D-Monapterin (distance: 1.939, cosine similarity: 0.897), a compound involved in pteridine metabolism. Fluticasone (distance: 2.108, cosine similarity: 0.890) and beclomethasone (distance: 2.192, cosine similarity: 0.882), both inhaled corticosteroids commonly used for asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, ranked third and fourth respectively. Additional top matches included CLODRONATE DISODIUM (distance: 2.299, cosine similarity: 0.883), an inhibitor of osteoclast activity, and sulfasalazine (distance: 2.380, cosine similarity: 0.875), a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug used for inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis. The prominence of anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs among the top matches is clinically significant, with corticosteroids recognized as established treatments in sepsis management, particularly for patients with septic shock. These findings suggest that the sepsis gene signature reflects inflammatory and immunomodulatory pathways that can be effectively targeted by multiple drug classes, supporting the biological rationale for therapeutic interventions in sepsis.

The drug matches identified from DSigDB demonstrate a clear pattern of anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and metabolic agents, with lipid mediators and corticosteroids representing dominant classes among top matches. This pattern aligns with the clinical understanding that sepsis involves dysregulated inflammatory responses mediated through lipid signaling pathways and that modulation of these responses through anti-inflammatory therapies may be beneficial. The identification of POVPC, a lipid mediator, as the top match suggests that lipid-derived mediators of inflammation may be key targets for sepsis therapy. The presence of multiple corticosteroid formulations (fluticasone, beclomethasone) indicates that the sepsis signature captures corticosteroid-responsive inflammatory pathways. Additional matches including parthenolide (distance: 2.431, cosine similarity: 0.863) with anti-inflammatory activity, DL-Mevalonic acid (distance: 2.495, cosine similarity: 0.871), ferrous sulfate (distance: 2.529, cosine similarity: 0.876), and dinoprostone (distance: 2.548, cosine similarity: 0.871) indicate that multiple immunomodulatory and metabolic mechanisms may be relevant to the sepsis response. These findings support the concept that drug repurposing for sepsis could target multiple points in the inflammatory cascade, including lipid mediators, corticosteroid-responsive pathways, and metabolic processes, potentially allowing for combination therapeutic approaches or alternative treatments for patients with specific sepsis phenotypes.

The DSigDB analysis provides a systematic approach to identifying drug repurposing candidates for sepsis based on gene expression signature similarity. The strong matches to corticosteroids validate the approach, as these drugs are already established in sepsis treatment, demonstrating that the embedding-based method can identify clinically relevant therapeutic agents. The identification of additional immunomodulatory drugs, such as sulfasalazine and tacrolimus, suggests potential alternative or complementary therapeutic strategies that warrant further investigation. These findings highlight the value of computational drug repurposing approaches for identifying novel treatment opportunities in sepsis, a condition where therapeutic options remain limited despite decades of research.
