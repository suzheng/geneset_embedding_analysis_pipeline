# Results

## Drug Perturbation Signatures from Gene Expression Omnibus Studies

To identify drug perturbations that modulate gene expression patterns similar to the sepsis signature, the sepsis-associated gene set was compared against the RummaGEO Drug Perturbations 2025 library, which contains 5,344 drug perturbation signatures derived from published Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) studies. These signatures represent experimentally observed gene expression changes following drug treatment in various experimental models, providing real-world evidence of drug effects on transcriptional programs. Similarity matching was performed using variance-weighted distance calculations across selected embedding dimensions, where smaller distance values indicate greater similarity between the sepsis signature and drug-induced expression patterns. The analysis revealed strong associations with several clinically relevant compounds that modulate immune and inflammatory responses, supporting potential drug repurposing opportunities for sepsis treatment.

The highest-ranked match was glutamine (distance: 3.002, cosine similarity: 0.769), an amino acid that serves as a central regulator of immune cell metabolism and modulates cytokine expression. Glutamine supplementation has been evaluated in critically ill and septic patients, with clinical trials showing mixed results and some studies reporting potential harm in certain patient populations. The match to a glutamine perturbation signature aligns with established knowledge that glutamine metabolism is altered in sepsis and that immune cell function depends on adequate glutamine availability. The second-ranked match was temozolomide (distance: 3.391, cosine similarity: 0.742), an alkylating chemotherapeutic agent used in brain tumor treatment. While not directly linked to sepsis biology, temozolomide can alter cytokine expression and cause severe immunosuppression that predisposes patients to serious infections, potentially explaining the observed signature overlap. The third-ranked match was bortezomib (distance: 3.448, cosine similarity: 0.739), a proteasome inhibitor used in multiple myeloma treatment that affects nuclear factor-kappa B signaling and inflammatory pathways. Preclinical sepsis models have shown divergent effects of proteasome inhibition depending on timing and dosing, suggesting context-dependent modulation of inflammatory responses.

Calcitriol, the active form of vitamin D, appeared twice in the top 10, with matches at distance 3.465 (cosine similarity: 0.737) and 3.602 (cosine similarity: 0.720) across different experimental conditions. Vitamin D receptor signaling has been repeatedly associated with sepsis susceptibility and outcomes, with vitamin D deficiency linked to increased infection risk and worse clinical outcomes in critically ill patients. The match to calcitriol perturbation signatures is consistent with established literature linking vitamin D status to immune function and sepsis pathophysiology. Ruxolitinib, a Janus kinase 1 and 2 inhibitor approved for myeloproliferative disorders, ranked fifth (distance: 3.496, cosine similarity: 0.720). Ruxolitinib downregulates JAK/STAT-mediated cytokine signaling, including interleukin-6 and interferon pathways, and preclinical models of hyperinflammation and sepsis have demonstrated reduction of key sepsis cytokines and amelioration of organ injury when administered appropriately. These findings suggest that the sepsis signature overlaps with JAK/STAT-regulated programs, identifying a potentially actionable therapeutic target.

Additional notable matches included curcumin (distance: 3.594, cosine similarity: 0.755), a natural product with anti-inflammatory properties derived from turmeric that has been investigated for various inflammatory conditions. Azacitidine (distance: 3.633, cosine similarity: 0.736) and decitabine (distance: 3.658, cosine similarity: 0.732), both DNA methyltransferase inhibitors used in hematologic malignancies, were also highly ranked. These epigenetic modifiers can alter immune cell function and cytokine production, potentially explaining their association with the sepsis signature. Berberine (distance: 3.712, cosine similarity: 0.722), a plant-derived alkaloid with antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, was another natural product match. While these compounds represent diverse drug classes, they share common themes of immune modulation and anti-inflammatory activity, suggesting that multiple mechanisms may be relevant to modulating the sepsis response.

The RummaGEO Drug Perturbations analysis provides evidence-based identification of drug repurposing candidates derived from real experimental data published in the scientific literature. Unlike curated drug signature databases, these perturbation signatures reflect actual observed effects of drug treatments in experimental models, providing additional validation of potential therapeutic targets. The identification of compounds with established links to sepsis biology, such as glutamine and calcitriol, validates the approach by demonstrating that the embedding-based method can identify clinically relevant associations. The discovery of less obvious candidates, such as ruxolitinib and bortezomib, suggests novel therapeutic opportunities that warrant further investigation. The diversity of drug classes represented among the top matches, including metabolic modulators, epigenetic modifiers, kinase inhibitors, and natural products, indicates that multiple therapeutic strategies may be viable for targeting the sepsis response. These findings highlight the value of computational approaches that leverage large-scale experimental data to identify drug repurposing opportunities, particularly for conditions like sepsis where therapeutic options remain limited and new treatment strategies are urgently needed.
