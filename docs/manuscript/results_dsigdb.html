Drug Repurposing Candidates Identified from DSigDB

To identify potential drug repurposing opportunities for sepsis treatment, the sepsis-associated gene signature was compared against the DSigDB library, which contains 4,025 drug-related gene signatures representing compounds with documented effects on gene expression. Drug matching was performed using variance-weighted distance calculations across 60 selected embedding dimensions, where smaller distance values indicate greater similarity between the sepsis signature and drug-induced expression patterns. The analysis revealed strong associations with several classes of anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs that are clinically relevant to sepsis pathophysiology.

The highest-ranked drug match was fluticasone (distance: 1.937, cosine similarity: 0.878), an inhaled corticosteroid commonly used for asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. This was followed by beclomethasone (distance: 2.121, cosine similarity: 0.852), another inhaled corticosteroid with similar anti-inflammatory properties. Additional corticosteroid matches included budesonide (distance: 2.762, cosine similarity: 0.798) and methylprednisolone (distance: 2.848, cosine similarity: 0.810), representing both inhaled and systemic corticosteroid formulations. The prominence of corticosteroids among the top matches is clinically significant, as corticosteroids are already used in sepsis treatment, particularly for patients with septic shock and relative adrenal insufficiency. These findings suggest that the sepsis gene signature reflects inflammatory pathways that are effectively modulated by corticosteroid therapy, supporting the biological rationale for corticosteroid use in sepsis management.

Beyond corticosteroids, several other anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory drugs showed strong matches to the sepsis signature. Sulfasalazine (distance: 2.165, cosine similarity: 0.846), a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug used for inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis, ranked fifth overall. Sulfasalazine has demonstrated anti-inflammatory effects through inhibition of nuclear factor-kappa B signaling and activation of nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 pathways, mechanisms that are relevant to sepsis pathophysiology. Tacrolimus monohydrate (distance: 2.732, cosine similarity: 0.792), an immunosuppressive calcineurin inhibitor used in organ transplantation, was also highly ranked, suggesting potential relevance of T-cell mediated immune responses in sepsis. Other notable matches included dinoprostone (distance: 2.536, cosine similarity: 0.820), a prostaglandin E2 analog with immunomodulatory properties, and parthenolide (distance: 2.612, cosine similarity: 0.807), a natural product with anti-inflammatory activity. These diverse drug classes indicate that multiple immunomodulatory mechanisms may be relevant to modulating the sepsis response.

The drug matches identified from DSigDB demonstrate a clear pattern of anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory agents, with corticosteroids representing the dominant class among top matches. This pattern aligns with the clinical understanding that sepsis involves dysregulated inflammatory responses, and that modulation of these responses through anti-inflammatory therapies may be beneficial. The identification of multiple corticosteroid formulations, including both inhaled and systemic agents, suggests that the sepsis signature captures corticosteroid-responsive pathways regardless of administration route. The presence of other immunomodulatory drugs, such as sulfasalazine and tacrolimus, indicates that alternative mechanisms of immune modulation beyond corticosteroid pathways may also be relevant. These findings support the concept that drug repurposing for sepsis could target multiple points in the inflammatory cascade, potentially allowing for combination therapeutic approaches or alternative treatments for patients who may not respond to or tolerate corticosteroid therapy.

The DSigDB analysis provides a systematic approach to identifying drug repurposing candidates for sepsis based on gene expression signature similarity. The strong matches to corticosteroids validate the approach, as these drugs are already established in sepsis treatment, demonstrating that the embedding-based method can identify clinically relevant therapeutic agents. The identification of additional immunomodulatory drugs, such as sulfasalazine and tacrolimus, suggests potential alternative or complementary therapeutic strategies that warrant further investigation. These findings highlight the value of computational drug repurposing approaches for identifying novel treatment opportunities in sepsis, a condition where therapeutic options remain limited despite decades of research. Future work should focus on experimental validation of these drug candidates, particularly those with strong matches that have not yet been evaluated in sepsis clinical trials, and on understanding the specific mechanisms by which these drugs may modulate the sepsis response.
