Results
To investigate the biology of sepsis, we focused on genes with established genetic associations with the disease. Specifically, we used a curated set of 23 genes derived from a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis of host genetic variants associated with sepsis susceptibility (Lu et al., Critical Care, 2019; PMCID: PMC6347778 ). That study synthesized 349 publications evaluating 405 variants across 172 genes, identifying 29 variants within 23 genes with significant associations with sepsis risk—spanning pattern recognition receptors, cytokines, and other immune mediators central to pathogen sensing and host defense.
Although the individual functions of these genes are established, genes often carry multiple biological roles, and some of those roles may not be immediately apparent. To obtain a broader picture of the disease, we therefore examined these 23 genes collectively—asking what they share and how they relate to one another as a group. The most widely used approach for this purpose is gene set enrichment analysis, which asks whether the disease-associated gene set significantly overlaps with other gene sets of known biological function. However, when working with a relatively small number of genes, as in the present study, direct overlap-based methods are particularly limited: the probability that a small gene set shares exact gene symbols with any given reference set is low even when the underlying biology is closely related.
To overcome this limitation, we employed a numerical representation of genes in which genes with similar biological functions are assigned similar numerical values, enabling comparison by functional proximity rather than by gene symbol identity. These representations were derived from GeneRAIN, a deep learning model trained on 410,000 gene expression samples, which ensures that the learned representations are generalisable and robust. Rather than asking whether two gene sets share the same genes, this approach asks whether they occupy similar regions of a biologically meaningful space—a question that can be answered even when gene lists do not overlap at all.
After mapping the sepsis-associated genes into the embedding space, we next identified which dimensions most reliably reflected shared biological characteristics of this gene set. Although each gene is represented across 200 numerical dimensions, not all dimensions contribute equally to defining the common functional profile of sepsis. Some dimensions capture biological attributes that are consistently shared across the genes, whereas others reflect heterogeneity and may introduce noise into similarity comparisons.
To prioritize dimensions that represent coherent biological features, we applied a variance-based filtering strategy. Dimensions were ranked according to the variability of the sepsis genes within each dimension. Those with lower variability indicate that the genes behave similarly in that feature, suggesting a shared biological property. We retained dimensions at or below the 30th percentile of variability, corresponding to 60 of the 200 total dimensions. The remaining 140 dimensions, which showed greater heterogeneity across genes, were excluded from downstream similarity analyses in order to reduce noise and enhance interpretability.

Of the 23 curated sepsis-associated genes, 21 were successfully mapped to the embedding reference space, corresponding to a coverage rate of 91.3%. This high mapping rate indicates that the vast majority of genetically supported sepsis susceptibility genes were represented in the learned biological space, providing a robust foundation for subsequent analyses.
The resulting sepsis embedding signature—defined by the collective functional position of these genes across the 60 selected dimensions—serves as a quantitative representation of the core biological architecture of sepsis susceptibility. This signature was then used for systematic comparison against curated libraries of pathways, immune cell types, diseases, and drug perturbation profiles to identify biological processes and therapeutic hypotheses that share functional similarity with the sepsis genetic architecture.
By restricting comparisons to biologically coherent dimensions and ensuring high gene coverage, we aimed to maximize signal-to-noise ratio and ensure that downstream similarity findings reflect shared biological mechanisms rather than statistical artifact.
