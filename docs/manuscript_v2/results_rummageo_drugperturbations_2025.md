

### Functional mapping of sepsis gene signature to drug perturbation signatures

To further identify potential therapeutic compounds associated with the sepsis molecular signature, we compared the sepsis-associated gene signature against 5,344 drug perturbation signatures from the RummaGEO Drug Perturbations library [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2024.101072], which contains experimentally derived transcriptional responses to drug exposure. Similarity was assessed in embedding space, where smaller distance values and higher cosine similarity indicate stronger alignment between perturbation-induced transcriptional patterns and the sepsis signature.

The highest-ranked match was glutamine (distance: 3.002, cosine similarity: 0.769), followed by temozolomide (distance: 3.391, cosine similarity: 0.742) and bortezomib (distance: 3.448, cosine similarity: 0.739). Calcitriol appeared twice among top-ranked signatures (distance: 3.465, cosine similarity: 0.737; and distance: 3.602, cosine similarity: 0.720), and ruxolitinib ranked fifth (distance: 3.496, cosine similarity: 0.720).

Additional high-ranking matches included curcumin (distance: 3.594, cosine similarity: 0.755), azacitidine (distance: 3.633, cosine similarity: 0.736), decitabine (distance: 3.658, cosine similarity: 0.732), and berberine (distance: 3.712, cosine similarity: 0.722).

Overall, top matching perturbations spanned compounds linked to metabolic regulation, epigenetic modulation, and cytokine signaling (Figure 6). Top drug perturbation matches are shown in Supplementary Table 4.
