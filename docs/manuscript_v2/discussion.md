# Discussion

The central challenge in sepsis research has never been a shortage of candidate targets—it has been an incomplete understanding of how those targets relate to one another within the broader immune landscape of a patient who is simultaneously fighting an infection and destroying their own tissues [https://doi.org/10.1038/nri.2017.36]. Our findings, generated through a gene representation-based approach applied to 23 genetically validated sepsis susceptibility genes, offer a functionally integrated view of this immune dysregulation and point toward therapeutic strategies that respect its complexity.

## The Dual Immune Signature Reflects Clinical Reality

The most clinically meaningful observation from this analysis is that the sepsis genetic signature simultaneously encodes features of active inflammation and immune exhaustion—not as competing signals, but as co-existing biological realities [https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(13)70001-X]. The strong functional similarity to Th17 cell populations and M1 macrophages on one hand, and to exhausted T cell programs on the other, mirrors exactly what intensivists observe at the bedside: patients mounting an inflammatory response vigorous enough to damage their own organs while simultaneously failing to clear the infection that triggered it [https://doi.org/10.1038/nri.2017.36].

This co-existence likely explains many large trial failures [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molmed.2014.01.007]. Studies targeting individual pro-inflammatory mediators enrolled patients without stratifying by immune state. If Th17-driven inflammation and T cell exhaustion co-exist in the same patient—encoded within the same genetic architecture, as our results suggest—then a therapy suppressing one without addressing the other would be expected to produce, at best, a neutral effect on mortality. The right therapeutic frame may therefore not be "suppress the cytokine storm" or "reverse the immunoparalysis" but rather "restore the regulatory balance between these systems."

The Reactome pathway analysis reinforces this picture. Top-ranked anti-inflammatory pathways, including IL-10 and IL-4/IL-13 signaling, likely reflect the immune system's own counter-regulatory attempts rather than a paradox. Their co-enrichment alongside TLR4 cascades, IL-6 family signaling, and chemokine receptor pathways paints a portrait of a system operating at high amplitude in both directions simultaneously [https://doi.org/10.1038/nri.2017.36].

## The IPF Connection Illuminates Organ Injury Mechanisms

One of the more striking findings is the molecular convergence between the sepsis signature and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. This overlap becomes intuitive when considered from the perspective of tissue injury and repair: both conditions involve progressive epithelial damage, aberrant tissue remodeling, and failure to restore normal organ architecture after an inflammatory insult. In sepsis, this is most directly relevant to acute respiratory distress syndrome, which shares key pathophysiological features with early IPF—including alveolar epithelial injury and dysregulated fibroblast activation [https://doi.org/10.1183/09031936.00196412]. The additional enrichment of nonspecific interstitial pneumonia signatures further reinforces the molecular affinity between septic lung injury and fibrotic remodeling. This raises the possibility that antifibrotic agents may warrant investigation in the recovery phase of sepsis-associated lung injury, when fibrotic remodeling becomes the dominant pathological process.

## JAK Inhibition as a Rationally Supported Therapeutic Strategy

The convergence of evidence from two independent drug signature libraries toward JAK-STAT pathway inhibition provides the most actionable clinical insight from this analysis. Ruxolitinib appeared as a high-ranking match in the RummaGEO drug perturbation analysis, and the Reactome enrichment of IL-6 and interferon signaling—both of which signal through the JAK-STAT axis [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-021-00791-1]—provides the mechanistic rationale for this alignment. JAK inhibition would be expected to attenuate both the inflammatory and the T cell exhaustion-promoting effects of sustained cytokine exposure, addressing both immune states simultaneously rather than one in isolation [https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms26136123].

This hypothesis is not without clinical precedent. JAK inhibitors have demonstrated efficacy in cytokine storm syndromes and in hyperinflammatory presentations of COVID-19, where baricitinib and ruxolitinib reduced mortality and ventilator requirements in selected patients [https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2031994]. The biological distance between those syndromes and sepsis-associated hyperinflammation is smaller than their diagnostic categories suggest, and the functional similarity identified here adds computational weight to the case for evaluating JAK inhibition in carefully selected sepsis patients with documented hyperinflammatory phenotypes.

The enrichment of metabolic pathway-targeting compounds—glutamine, calcitriol, mevalonic acid—in the drug analysis reflects the growing recognition that immune dysfunction in sepsis is shaped not only by cytokine dysregulation but by profound metabolic reprogramming in activated and exhausted immune cells, an axis that deserves greater attention in therapeutic development [https://doi.org/10.1097/CCE.0000000000000061, https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1679493].

## Methodological Considerations and Limitations

A key strength of this analysis is that it achieves biologically coherent results from a relatively small input gene set precisely because embedding-based comparisons do not require gene symbol overlap to detect functional similarity. The variance-filtering step, which retained embedding dimensions reflecting shared biological properties across sepsis genes, further enhanced signal specificity, and the 91.3% gene coverage rate confirms that the functional signature is not driven by a small subset of over-represented genes.

Nonetheless, the input gene set reflects associations with sepsis susceptibility rather than severity or specific clinical phenotypes, and genes governing susceptibility may overlap only partially with those driving organ injury or mortality [https://doi.org/10.1097/SHK.0000000000002643]. Drug signature libraries also represent transcriptional responses measured under specific experimental conditions that may not fully recapitulate the in vivo septic environment. Findings should therefore be interpreted as mechanistically grounded hypotheses requiring prospective experimental and clinical validation.

## Conclusion

The sepsis genetic architecture encodes simultaneous features of Th17-driven inflammation and T cell exhaustion—a combination that explains the failure of unidirectional immunomodulatory strategies and that demands therapeutic approaches capable of restoring immune balance. The molecular convergence with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis highlights tissue remodeling as an underappreciated axis of sepsis pathophysiology. And the consistent nomination of JAK-STAT inhibition across independent analyses, grounded in the pathway-level enrichment of IL-6 and interferon signaling, offers a mechanistically coherent and clinically testable therapeutic hypothesis. For the clinician at the bedside, these results reaffirm that sepsis immune dysregulation is not a single failure but a systemic collapse of immune regulation across multiple axes—and that therapeutic success will likely require addressing that complexity in full.
